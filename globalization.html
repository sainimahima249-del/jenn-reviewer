Enrichment Activity — Globalization
A world
stitched together
How trade, technology, and shared culture pulled the planet into one connected system — and what that connection costs and creates.
① Definition
What globalization means
Globalization is the growing interconnection of the world's economies, cultures, and populations — driven by cross-border trade, investment, technology, and the movement of people and information.
"The process by which the world becomes a single, interdependent system — economically, socially, and culturally."
② Causes
What drives it
Four forces accelerated the shift from separate national economies to one interlinked global system.
01
Technology & the internet
Instant communication and data transfer erased distance as a barrier to doing business.
02
Trade liberalization
Lower tariffs and free-trade agreements made cross-border commerce cheaper and faster.
03
Transportation advances
Container shipping and air freight cut the cost and time of moving goods worldwide.
04
Multinational corporations
Companies expanded operations across countries, linking markets and labor forces together.
③ Effects
What it changes
Globalization brings real gains alongside real costs — the two sides usually arrive together.
Positive
  • Wider access to goods, jobs, and markets
  • Faster spread of knowledge and innovation
  • Lower prices through global competition
  • Cultural exchange and shared ideas
Negative
  • Local industries struggle against global competitors
  • Wealth gaps widen between and within nations
  • Traditional cultures can be diluted
  • Economic shocks spread across borders faster
④ Effects on communication
How the world talks now
Communication changed the fastest of anything globalization touched — collapsing the time between "I have news" and "the world knows."
Before
Letters and calls limited by distance, cost, and time zones.
→
Shift
Internet and mobile networks make contact instant and near-free.
→
Now
Social media links strangers across countries into one shared conversation.
