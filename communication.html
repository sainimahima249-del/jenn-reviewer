💬 PROCESS AND ELEMENTS OF COMMUNICATION — EXAM REVIEWER 💬

1. Introduction

Communication is to the organization as blood is to the body — the success of all management functions depends on effective communication. The communication process can be simple or complex depending on the situation, and specific elements must be present for it to succeed.

2. The 8 Elements of Communication

1. Sender — the person who sends ideas to another person. Example: a manager informing subordinates about a new product is the sender.

2. Message — the idea, feeling, suggestion, guideline, order, or content meant to be communicated. Example: the introduction of a new product.

3. Encoding — the process of converting the idea or message into symbols, words, actions, or diagrams.

4. Media — the medium or route through which the encoded message travels from sender to receiver (face-to-face, letters, radio, TV, email, etc.). Example: a manager announcing a new product via presentation in a meeting.

5. Decoding — translating the encoded message into language understandable by the receiver.

6. Receiver — the person to whom the message is sent. Example: subordinates are receivers.

7. Feedback — the response by the receiver; it marks the completion of the communication process.

8. Noise — any hindrance in the communication process that reduces accuracy. Can occur at any step. Examples: disturbance in telephone lines, an inattentive receiver, improper decoding of message.

3. Communication Process Diagram

Flow: Sender → Encoding → Message (via Channel/Media) → Decoding → Receiver → Response → Feedback (back to Sender). Noise can interfere at the Channel/Media stage.

4. Communication Process — 6 Steps

1) Sender formulates the message. 2) Sender encodes/translates the message (symbols, words, actions, diagrams, pictures). 3) Sender selects a channel/medium (face-to-face, letters, radio, TV, email). 4) Message is received by the receiver. 5) Receiver decodes the message to draw meaning. 6) Receiver sends a response to the sender; if there's confusion, clarification is sought.

5. General Principles of Effective Communication

Since communication is a two-way process, these principles apply to both oral and written communication:

1. Know your purpose in communicating — are you communicating to inform, entertain, or persuade? There's usually one dominant objective.

2. Know your audience — consider their age, educational background, profession, and culture, as it dictates your speaking/writing style.

3. Know your topic — you communicate because you want to share something; use techniques that catch the audience's attention.

4. Adjust your speech or writing to the context of the situation — the environment determines the kind of language used.

5. Work on the feedback given to you — take criticism kindly; constructive criticism helps you improve long-term.

6. Synthesis

Communication follows a process made up of 8 elements: Sender, Message, Encoding, Media, Decoding, Receiver, Feedback, and Noise. Principles must be considered throughout this process to ensure effectiveness.
